In this single-center cohort, neoadjuvant trastuzumab plus pertuzumab was associated with a higher pCR rate than trastuzumab alone after measured characteristics were balanced with overlap weighting. The size of the difference was similar to that observed in randomized trials, but the evidence for EFS and OS was inconclusive.

In NeoSphere, adding pertuzumab to trastuzumab and docetaxel increased the breast pCR rate from 29.0% to 45.8%,[@gianni2012neosphere] and the Asian PEONY trial reported a similar increase in total pCR,[@shao2020peony] with five-year EFS of 84.8% versus 73.7% in its descriptive final analysis.[@huang2024peonyfinal] Real-world studies from Turkey, Italy, China, and Spain have also reported higher pCR rates with dual blockade,[@bilici2023her2path; @fabbri2023neopearl; @cheng2022csbrs015; @diazredondo2019pam50] and a meta-analysis of 18 real-world studies estimated a pooled OR of 1.81 (95% CI, 1.56–2.09).[@matsas2026metaanalysis] Our estimate is consistent with these findings.

Our results also show how strongly treatment era can influence real-world comparisons. The two regimens were used in largely different years, and the crude pCR rates were almost identical because the overall pCR rate was lower in recent years, when nearly all patients received dual blockade. Within each period, however, pCR was more frequent with dual blockade. We could not determine why the pCR rate was lower in recent years; possible explanations include changes in patient selection, chemotherapy backbones, and pathologic assessment, such as more thorough examination of lymph nodes that identifies isolated tumor cells as residual disease. Overlap weighting addresses this problem by focusing the comparison on patients who were treated when either regimen was plausible, mainly those treated in 2016–2019, and avoids the extreme weights that inverse probability weighting would have produced.[@li2019extreme] The estimate should therefore be interpreted as the effect in patients for whom both regimens were realistic options.

The EFS and OS results were inconclusive. Follow-up was shorter in the dual-blockade group, events were few, and post-neoadjuvant treatment changed over time. After the KATHERINE trial showed that adjuvant T-DM1 improves invasive disease-free survival and OS in patients with residual disease,[@vonminckwitz2019katherine; @geyer2025katherineos] T-DM1 was used mainly in the dual-blockade era in our cohort. In APHINITY, adjuvant pertuzumab did not improve OS at a median follow-up of 8.4 years,[@loibl2024aphinityos] and pCR has not been validated as a trial-level surrogate for EFS or OS.[@cortazar2014ctneobc] A higher pCR rate should therefore not be assumed to translate into a proportional survival benefit.

We applied the international definition of pCR, under which residual isolated tumor cells are considered residual disease.[@provenzano2015bignabcg; @bossuyt2024iccr] Low-volume residual nodal disease after neoadjuvant chemotherapy, including isolated tumor cells, has been associated with worse disease-free and overall survival than node-negative disease,[@wong2019itc] which supports this stricter definition.

The strengths of this study include an analysis plan that was finalized before outcomes were examined, validation and correction of the source data before analysis, a design that addressed the limited overlap between treatment eras, and several sensitivity analyses. This study also has limitations. First, it was a single-center retrospective study, and residual confounding by unmeasured factors associated with treatment era cannot be excluded; an unmeasured confounder associated with both treatment and pCR by an OR of approximately {{evalue_pcr}} could explain the observed association. Second, the effective sample size after weighting was small, so the estimates were imprecise, and the matched analysis, which retained only {{n_s2_pairs}} pairs, yielded a smaller estimate. Third, EFS did not include contralateral breast cancer or second primary cancers because these events were not recorded, and loss to follow-up may have differed between treatment eras. Fourth, data on other active malignancies and on participation in clinical trials were unavailable. Finally, missing covariate data were handled by multiple imputation, which assumes that the data were missing at random.

In conclusion, in routine practice, adding pertuzumab to neoadjuvant trastuzumab and chemotherapy was associated with a higher pCR rate, consistent with randomized evidence. The result depended on adjustment for treatment era, and the data were insufficient to determine the effects on EFS or OS.
